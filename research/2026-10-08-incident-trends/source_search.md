# Source and prior-work search

Search date: October 8, 2026. The search used web search and direct reads of primary sources. It is not a systematic literature review.

Initial queries included:

- AI Incident Database download dataset incidents severity taxonomy dates 2026
- OECD AI incidents monitor severity classification incidents hazards methodology

The search then followed official archive, taxonomy, methods, and paper links. Separate Codex agents checked the severity fields and related work.

## Sources retained

1. AIID archive index and the January 5 and October 5, 2026 downloads. Purpose: fixed incident records and classification files.
2. AIID terms and CC BY-SA 4.0. Purpose: attribution and data reuse limits.
3. CSET taxonomy repository and AIID CSETv1 page. Purpose: field meaning and the end of annotation work.
4. Dartmouth history page. Purpose: the 1956 reference date for AI as a field.
5. Partnership on AI launch announcement. Purpose: database launch in 2020.
6. AIID incident 27. Purpose: the earliest database date and the CSET AI-definition caveat.
7. Stanford AI Index 2026, Responsible AI summary. Purpose: prior count analysis and a dataset-version warning.
8. Mylius, Scalable AI Incident Classification, April 14, 2025. Purpose: prior severity analysis, category comparability, and validation limits.
9. Abraham et al., arXiv:2604.19914v1. Purpose: reporting and exposure limits. The abstract, introduction, and relevant limitations were read.
10. Mengesha et al., arXiv:2604.21412v3. Purpose: prior monitoring framework. The abstract and relevant methods text were read.

URLs are included in report.md and references.bib. No claim depends on a search snippet alone.

## Sources considered but not pooled

- [OECD AIM methodology](https://oecd.ai/en/incidents-methodology): a separate media-monitoring system with different event grouping and a method change. Its counts were not merged with AIID.
- [OECD 2026 trend report](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/02/trends-in-ai-incidents-and-hazards-reported-by-the-media_7c824ca9/4f5ff43c-en.pdf): close prior work on media themes. Its quantitative results were not reused here.
- [MIT AI Incident Tracker](https://airisk.mit.edu/ai-incident-tracker): automated severity classifications with stated validation limits. These scores were not mixed with CSETv0 or CSETv1.
- Other incident lists found in search: not used because their scope and classification rules differ from the selected archive.

## Original contribution claim

The study adds calculations for two pinned snapshots, a change decomposition by incident ID, and a severity-coverage sensitivity check. The methods are established. Similar analyses may exist. This search does not establish global novelty, a first discovery, or a complete literature review.
